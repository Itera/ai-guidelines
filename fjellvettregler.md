# Itera AI-Fjellvettregler

## Before you head out

### 1. Know the rules before you head out
Read Itera’s [AI Use Policy at Itera](https://iteracloud.sharepoint.com/sites/framework/Itera%20Framework/AI%20use%20Policy%20at%20Itera.aspx) and get to know the main regulations. Ask before you start, not after.

### 2. Tell someone where you’re going
Agree on AI use with the customer before you start. Settle which tools you’ll use, what data the AI may see, and whether AI costs are billed to the customer or included in Itera’s delivery.

### 3. Use approved equipment
Only use AI tools approved for Itera or customer PCs. Before you use a model, check who owns it and where it’s hosted. Vet third-party skills, agents and packages before you install them.

## Safety on the trip

### 4. Pack only what you can afford to lose
Never put sensitive data in prompts or context. That includes personal data, competitively sensitive information, and data that must not be used for training or made public.

### 5. Give agents their own rope
Agents shouldn’t use your account, because you usually have more access than they need. Give them their own accounts with only the access the task requires.

Instructions in a prompt are not guardrails, since prompt injection can override them. If an agent technically *can* do something it shouldn’t, that’s not safe enough. Use hard limits like permissions, sandboxes and approval steps, especially in auto mode.

### 6. Know your provisions
Know what the models cost and check your usage regularly. Set a budget with alerts so you don’t run dry during something critical, like a production release.

Match the model and effort level to the task, because a simple task doesn’t need the most expensive model. [DeepSWE](https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/) can help you choose.

## Coming home safely

### 7. You own what you bring home
You are responsible for everything AI produces for you. Review code against the customer’s quality rules, and question suggestions that push particular products.

Keep key decisions with people, such as architecture, programming language and handover to operations.

### 8. It’s no shame to turn back
If the AI goes in circles, or you no longer understand what it’s doing, stop. Step back and either rethink the task or do it yourself. Your judgment is the best equipment you have.

### 9. Hike as a team
The biggest return comes when the whole team and its processes use AI, not just individuals. Share setups, skills and lessons learned.

### 10. Check that the trip was worth it
Measure the effect with something like the DORA metrics. That shows whether AI actually improves delivery, so you keep what works and drop what doesn’t.
